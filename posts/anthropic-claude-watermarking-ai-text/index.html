Anthropic Watermarks All Claude Outputs — Invisibly, Globally

Every text response from Claude now carries a hidden statistical signature designed to survive copying and editing. The policy went live on August 2 in response to the EU AI Act, but applies worldwide.

By Editorial Team August 16, 2026  ·  4 min read
#AI#Anthropic#Claude#Watermarking#EU AI Act#C2PA

Starting August 2, 2026, every piece of text generated by a Claude model carries an invisible watermark. Anthropic activated the feature to comply with the EU AI Act's transparency requirements, but chose to apply it globally — across the Claude API, claude.ai, Claude Code, and all other Claude-powered surfaces — rather than restricting it to European users.

The watermark is not a visible label, a hidden character, or a metadata field. Instead, it works by introducing a subtle statistical bias into the model's word-selection process during generation — a technique similar to Google DeepMind's SynthID-Text system. The resulting text reads identically to an unwatermarked response, but its word-choice distribution carries a detectable pattern. Because the bias is woven into the text itself rather than appended to it, the watermark survives copying, pasting, and light editing.

What It Can and Cannot Survive

Anthropic has been candid about the system's limitations. Heavy rewriting, translation into another language, or significant paraphrasing can degrade or eliminate the detectable signal. The watermark is therefore most reliable for detecting unmodified or lightly altered AI-generated content — the kind that appears in academic submissions, news aggregators, or social media posts where the original text is reproduced largely intact.

Watermarking does not solve AI-generated misinformation. It makes a specific class of unmodified AI output detectable. That is a meaningful but narrow capability.

Image and File Provenance via C2PA

For image and supported file outputs, Anthropic has adopted a different approach. Files generated by Claude now include digitally signed provenance metadata following the C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) standard. Unlike the statistical text watermark, C2PA metadata is cryptographically signed and tied to the specific model version that produced the file. It can be read by any software that supports the open standard, including Adobe Photoshop, and is already used by several major news organisations to verify the origin of images. Anthropic has stated it will publish tools and documentation to help users and third parties detect both types of marking, though public detection utilities were still in development as of this week.

The policy applies to Claude models released on or after August 2. Older models are being updated on a rolling basis. The move makes Anthropic one of the first major AI providers to implement watermarking as a standard feature across all outputs globally, ahead of any regulatory deadline requiring it in non-EU jurisdictions.
